# Pitch Video Script — Gnani Lecture-to-Learning Platform

**Purpose**: a short promotional video (~60–90 seconds) that explains what Gnani
does and why it matters, in plain language, for people who have never seen the
product. Use this as the narration script and scene brief when generating the
video (e.g. with Higgsfield) or when recording it yourself.

**Audience**: students and teachers — primarily Indian college and school
students who record lectures and then struggle to revise them. Also useful for
judges or evaluators at a hackathon / product showcase, so the script avoids
jargon and never assumes they know the codebase.

**Voice**: a friendly teacher talking to one student. Natural, warm, second
person ("you"). Short sentences. No marketing buzzwords, no "revolutionary",
no emoji, no on-screen bullet storms. Everything said must be true of the
product as it stands today.

**Tone references**: calm explainer, like a teacher summarising a chapter in
two minutes. Slightly conversational, never salesy.

---

## Scene 1 — The problem (0:00–0:15)

**Visual**: a student at a desk with hours of recorded lectures, scrolling
through a long audio timeline, looking lost.

**Narration (teacher voice):**

> You record every lecture. But when exam week comes, you never have time to
> listen to all of it again. Notes are either missing, or someone else's — and
> they don't match what your teacher actually said.

---

## Scene 2 — What Gnani does (0:15–0:35)

**Visual**: the upload screen, then the pipeline graphic: recording in,
transcript, then a clean notes document appearing section by section.

**Narration:**

> Gnani turns a recording into study material. You upload the lecture, and it
> transcribes the whole class, splits it into topics, and writes you proper
> notes — a summary, an explanation for every section, and the key terms at
> the end. With timestamps, so you can jump straight back to the original
> moment in the audio.

---

## Scene 3 — The tutor (0:35–1:00)

**Visual**: the chat screen — a student asks a question in Hindi or Hinglish,
the answer appears with segment citations; then a shot of the voice session,
the student speaking and the tutor replying. Show an out-of-scope question
getting the honest "not covered" reply.

**Narration:**

> Then you can actually talk to it. Ask a question out loud — in Hindi,
> Hinglish, Gujarati, or English — and the tutor answers in the same language
> you used. Every answer comes from your lecture and shows you the exact
> segments it used. And if the class never covered it, it says so plainly
> instead of guessing. That honesty is the point: you can trust it, because
> it refuses to teach you anything your teacher didn't say.

---

## Scene 4 — Revision loop (1:00–1:15)

**Visual**: the flashcards screen — a card flips, the student grades it, the
"round complete" state.

**Narration:**

> And when it's time to revise, Gnani turns the lecture into flashcards. You
> grade yourself, and it brings each card back right before you'd forget it.

---

## Scene 5 — Close (1:15–1:30)

**Visual**: the three screens side by side (notes, chat, flashcards), then the
product name.

**Narration:**

> One recording in. Notes, a tutor, and a revision deck out. Gnani — your
> lecture, made studyable.

---

## Generation notes

- Keep narration under 220 words total so the video stays inside 90 seconds at
  a natural speaking pace (~140 wpm).
- One voice throughout: warm, mid-paced, teacher-like. No announcer energy.
- On-screen text: only the product name and at most one short label per scene
  (e.g. "Notes", "Ask anything", "Revise"). Do not subtitle the whole
  narration on screen — it clutters the frames.
- All four scenes must show the real UI where possible (screenshots or screen
  recordings composited in) rather than invented interfaces; a pitch video
  that shows a UI the product doesn't have fails its own audience.
